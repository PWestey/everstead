# Everstead: story outline (draft for approval)

Status: **draft**. Nothing here is written into the game yet. Once the owner approves (or marks up) this outline,
chapters get written as dialogue trees in the usual format, following `docs/WRITING-BRIEF.md`.

The rule for everything below: **the story explains the habit loop, it never replaces it.** Every chapter is unlocked
by things the player already does (total level, a Map boss). No new currencies, no penalties for a missed day.

---

## 1. Premise

Somewhere between worlds lives Morrow, the Thief of Days. He eats the things people mean to do and never get round
to: the call put off, the run skipped, the “I’ll start Monday.” He grew fat on that. Then he got greedy.

He tore rifts between worlds and reached for the most driven people he could find, the ones with the most
tomorrows left to steal. He grabbed them at the instant they were about to act, because that’s when a person is
richest. A Jedi mid-step. A soldier with a radio in his hand. A thief with a coin in the air.

He didn’t get them. On the edge of Gielinor, in a farming village called Everstead, an old fire called the Hearth
caught them first. It burns on kept promises, and it has been close to going out for years.

Then you moved in. Your days are real, and your promises count. Keep them, and the Hearth burns. Burn it bright
enough and the heroes wake up, the rifts close, and Morrow goes hungry.

---

## 2. Morrow, the Thief of Days

**Who he is.** Not a demon, not a god. Morrow is what’s left of every “later” that never came, grown big enough
to want things. He looks like a tall, pleasant man in a grey travelling coat whose face you can never quite
remember afterwards. He is polite, reasonable and sympathetic. He never shouts. He’s the friend who says you look
tired and should take the night off, every night.

**What he wants.** Tomorrows. A promise that was meant and then dropped is a meal. A hero’s whole unfinished story
is a feast, which is why he went after the cast. He wants them back, and he wants the Hearth out, because it’s the
one thing in any world that has ever taken food off his plate.

**His rules (and weaknesses):**
- **He can only take what’s put off.** A promise kept is ash to him; he can’t touch it. A promise broken outright
  isn’t much better. What he lives on is the soft middle: “not today.”
- **He can’t cross the Hearth’s light.** He can’t set foot in Everstead. He speaks through rifts, sends his Blights,
  and leans on the edges of the map where the light is thin.
- **He never lies.** He doesn’t need to. Everything he says is true and a little bit beside the point
  (“You *could* do it tomorrow”).
- **Small beats big.** He’s strong against grand plans, because grand plans are mostly future. He’s weak against
  something small done right now. A five-minute walk does him more harm than a New Year’s resolution.
- **He can’t take time that’s been given away.** Hours spent on a wife, a kid, a friend are gone from his reach.
  That’s why Marriage and Fatherhood fronts matter so much to him and why Neglect is his favourite herald.
- **He can’t be killed, only starved.** The ending closes his rifts and seals him out. He doesn’t die, because
  “later” never does. He just gets thinner.

**How he shows up in play:**
- **Chapter scenes**, speaking through a rift (a grey shimmer at the screen edge, no portrait, just a voice in
  the chatbox).
- **Blight title cards.** Each week’s Blight arrives with one line from him, the herald’s “sender’s note.”
- **Rare evening line** when dailies are still open (once a day at most, switchable off with cheers). Never a
  penalty, never guilt. Just a reasonable voice the player gets to ignore by ticking something off.
- **Arena and Spire flavour** (see section 4).

**Voice sample:**
- “Sit down. It’ll keep. Everything keeps, with me.”
- “I’m not asking you to quit. Just don’t start. I’m very patient about the difference.”
- “Every one of them was about to do something wonderful. I simply got there first.”

## 3. The Hearth, and why the player matters

The Hearth is a ring of old black stones in Everstead’s square, older than the village. The village was built
around it. Nobody lit it; it just burns, and it burns on one thing: **promises that were kept.** Anyone’s. A
farmer who said he’d mend the fence and did. A mother who said she’d be home for supper and was.

Over the years the village thinned out and the Hearth fell to embers. The Gielinor locals who stayed (Maggie and
her cauldron, the Wise Old Man, a few others) kept it alive on small things, barely.

When Morrow’s rifts opened, the Hearth did what it’s always done: it held on to anyone who still had promises in
them. It caught the heroes mid-pull. But it was too weak to wake them. They lie in its light like banked coals,
frozen in the moment they were taken.

**Why the player matters:** the heroes can’t fuel it themselves. Their time stopped when Morrow grabbed them; their
promises belong to stories that are paused. They literally have no tomorrows here to keep. **The player is the only
one in Everstead whose days actually move.** Every real habit kept is a promise kept, and it goes straight into the
fire. That’s the whole game, in fiction:

- Each habit feeds the Hearth (XP, gold, sigils: the fire’s light spilling out into the village).
- Points in a life area wake the heroes tied to it (the existing join queue).
- Total level is how bright the Hearth burns; armour tiers are the Hearth’s light hardening around you.

The heroes don’t owe the player anything, and they know it. Most of them wake up irritated, confused or ready to
fight. They stay because the player keeps showing up, which is the one thing every one of them respects.

## 4. How each system and mode fits

**Core loop**
- **Habits (the Journal):** promises kept. The Hearth’s only fuel.
- **XP, skills and total level:** how bright the Hearth burns around you. RuneScape level-ups are the fire
  flaring.
- **Armour tiers:** the Hearth’s light settles on whoever feeds it. Bronze to Saradomin is the fire trusting you more.
- **Fellows (recruit, level, star):** heroes the Hearth was holding, woken by habits in their area. Gold and sigils
  help them find their strength in a strange world.
- **Family (welcome, bond, blessings):** the same, and the reason the village starts to feel like home. Bond is time
  given, which Morrow can’t touch.
- **Buildings and Momentum:** the village coming back to life. Momentum is the whole village warming at once when
  you touch every area in a week.
- **Quests:** each Fellow working out who they are here, with the player’s help. One per hero, as written.
- **Moments and Cheers:** the heroes noticing. Cheers are someone from that area seeing you keep a promise.
- **The Archives (Codex):** the things the village refuses to forget. Morrow steals tomorrows and lets yesterdays
  fade; the Archives keep the lessons sharp. Mastered cards warm the buildings.

**Expedition Map**
- **Roams:** a daily habit kept is a day where the Hearth’s light reaches a little further. Roams spend that light
  outside the village.
- **Regions:** where Morrow’s rifts bled into Gielinor. Each region’s trouble got worse because a rift opened near it.
- **Region bosses:** each one has been feeding off, or guarding, a rift. Beating the boss closes it. The region perk
  is the Hearth’s light settling over newly safe ground.

**Blights**
- **The weekly Blight:** one of Morrow’s seven heralds (Sloth, Distraction, Doubt, Neglect, Pride, Excess, Burnout)
  climbs out of a rift and leans on the village for a week. It’s hurt only by promises kept that week, double from
  the areas it hates.
- **Daily strikes:** the herald probes once a day. Each day’s habits are your answer. It comes back stronger next
  round because Morrow remembers what didn’t work.

**Upcoming modes**
- **Errands:** the Family keep the village running. They head out on jobs into Gielinor (supplies, messages, rumours
  of rifts) while the Fellows hold the line. You send them; they come back with stories and spoils.
- **The Rift (daily ladder):** one small rift tears open at the village edge every morning. Push into it as far as
  you can that day. The ladder is everyone’s best depth; it seals itself at midnight either way.
- **The Eight Fronts (weekly):** Morrow probes all eight life areas at once. Each front is defended by the heroes of
  that area, and holds by that week’s habits in it. Lose a front and nothing breaks; it just reopens next week.
- **The Trials (8 rule rooms):** the Hearth’s own tests, built into the old stones under the square. Each room obeys
  one rule taken from one life area (“nothing hurts in this room except the slow way”). Pass all eight and the Hearth
  trusts you with more.
- **The Spire (endless climb):** Morrow builds with what he steals. The Spire is a tower made of other people’s
  unfinished tomorrows, standing just past the Wilderness ditch. You can climb it forever because he never stops
  adding floors.
- **The Arena (franchise rival teams):** when Morrow lost his catch, he made copies out of what he could hold:
  echoes of the heroes’ old comrades and enemies, built from their unfinished business. They fight like the real
  thing and talk like the real thing, slightly wrong. The heroes find this deeply unpleasant. Beating an echo frees
  the bit of tomorrow it was made from.
- **Draft Gauntlet (roguelike run):** Morrow’s favourite game, and a bet. He deals you heroes at random off the top
  of a deck and dares you to see how far a stranger’s team can go. Win, and he pays up. He always pays; he never lies.

## 5. The main story: eight chapters

Each chapter is one dialogue sequence (3–5 scenes, the usual full-screen backdrop, portraits and RS chatbox) plus
a short epilogue line in the village. Unlocks use what the player already earns. Choices set flags that colour later
lines and pick a small reward; they don’t branch the plot.

### Chapter 1: Embers
- **Unlock:** first habit completed (total level 8).
- **Beats:** You arrive at a run-down farm on the edge of a run-down village. In the square, a ring of black stones
  holds a fire about the size of a candle, and lying around it are a dozen people who look like they’ve been
  painted mid-step. Maggie, stirring her cauldron, tells you the Hearth’s been dying for years and asks what you
  came here to do. You tell her, and the moment you do the first thing on your list, the fire jumps. Darrow wakes
  up mid-swing, swings at nothing, and demands to know which Gold did this.
- **Featuring:** Maggie, Darrow, the Wise Old Man (in passing, “Took you long enough”).
- **Choice:** Maggie asks why you came. *“To get my life in order.”* / *“Honestly? I keep saying I’ll start.”* /
  *“Somebody has to keep this fire going.”* Sets the player’s opening motive, quoted back to them in Chapter 8.

### Chapter 2: The House on the Hill
- **Unlock:** Count Draynor defeated + total level 60.
- **Beats:** With Draynor’s basement quiet, the heroes find what the Count was really doing down there: drinking
  from a grey tear in the cellar wall, and it wasn’t blood. Geralt recognises the smell of something old and patient.
  The rift closes, and for the first time a voice comes through it before it goes: polite, interested, a little
  amused that the Hearth is still lit. Obi-Wan names what everyone’s thinking: someone brought them here on purpose.
- **Featuring:** Geralt, Obi-Wan, Anna (Vampyre of Morytania, who knew the Count and is not sorry).
- **Choice:** The voice asks your name. *Give it.* / *Refuse.* / *“Who’s asking?”* Morrow uses your name from here on
  if you gave it; if not, he calls you “the farmer,” which Geralt approves of.

### Chapter 3: Something Under Falador
- **Unlock:** Giant Mole defeated + total level 150 (Iron).
- **Beats:** The Giant Mole wasn’t mad, it was fleeing. It had been digging away from a rift under Falador Park that
  was slowly turning the city’s gardens grey. Queen Ellamaria hosts the heroes at the palace and asks the question
  nobody has wanted to ask: can they go home? Steve, who already lost seventy years once, quietly says he doesn’t
  know if he wants to find out. Tavi points out that if Morrow pulled them in through rifts, there might be a way
  back out the same way, and that’s exactly what Morrow will dangle.
- **Featuring:** Steve, Tavi, Kitai, Queen Ellamaria.
- **Choice:** Steve asks what you’d do in his place. *“Go home. Finish it.”* / *“Stay. Build something here.”* /
  *“Decide later.”* (Morrow, faintly, through the floor: “Good answer.”) Sets a flag Steve returns to in Chapter 7.

### Chapter 4: Six Brothers
- **Unlock:** The Barrows Brothers defeated + total level 220.
- **Beats:** In the Barrows crypts the heroes meet the first echo: a copy of someone they know, standing guard over
  the rift, talking like an old friend and getting the details wrong. Logan takes it worst. The thing has a face he
  hasn’t seen in a long time. Revan works it out: Morrow can’t make people, only shapes out of what they left
  undone, and every echo is built from a regret. This chapter opens The Arena.
- **Featuring:** Logan, Revan, Din.
- **Choice:** After the fight, Logan wants to be left alone. *Leave him be.* / *Sit with him and say nothing.* /
  *Hand him a drink.* Each gets a different Logan line in the epilogue; “sit with him” earns a later Moment where he
  talks about it.

### Chapter 5: Sixty-Two Waves
- **Unlock:** TzTok-Jad defeated + total level 280 (Mithril).
- **Beats:** The Fight Cave turns out to be a rift that’s been throwing waves at the TzHaar for years, and Jad was
  the biggest thing it ever spat out. Kaladin understands the shape of it at once: Morrow doesn’t need to win a
  fight, he just needs you to get tired of having it every day. Ragnar, who was bred for exactly this kind of
  endless war, says the only way through is to stop counting and keep standing. With the cave sealed, a smaller
  rift starts opening at the village edge each morning. That’s The Rift ladder, and The Spire appears on the
  horizon, far off past the Wilderness.
- **Featuring:** Kaladin and Syl, Ragnar, Kelsier (who keeps calling the Spire “the vault”).
- **Choice:** Syl asks what you do on the days you don’t feel like it. *“Do it anyway.”* / *“Do a smaller version.”* /
  *“Ask somebody to make me.”* All three are “right”; each hero responds in their own way, and Syl approves loudly of
  the second.

### Chapter 6: The Hive of Tomorrows
- **Unlock:** Kalphite Queen defeated + total level 360 (Rune).
- **Beats:** Under the desert, the hive was full of something the Kalphites had been hoarding for Morrow: little
  glowing cells, each one a day someone meant to use and didn’t. Kelsier wants to rob it, obviously. Vin points out
  you can’t give a stolen day back to whoever lost it. Tony works out that you *can* burn them, and that the Hearth
  would take them as fuel. The heroes argue it out properly, the first real disagreement in the village, and the
  player decides.
- **Featuring:** Kelsier, Vin, Tony, Jaina.
- **Choice:** *Burn the stolen days in the Hearth* (big one-off boost to the fire, Kelsier delighted) / *Break them
  open and let them go* (Vin’s way; nothing gained, Morrow loses them too) / *Leave them sealed* (Jaina’s caution).
  Morrow comments on all three in Chapter 8. No option is wrong.

### Chapter 7: The Offer
- **Unlock:** Dagannoth Kings defeated + total level 440 (Dragon).
- **Beats:** On Waterbirth Island, with three kings down and a rift closing, Morrow stops pretending he isn’t
  interested and makes an offer through it. He’ll send every hero back to the exact moment he took them, story
  intact. All he wants in return is the Hearth to go out. He isn’t lying; he never does. Dalinar, who has turned
  down a god’s bargain before, says the offer is real and the price is the point. Thor just wants to hit something.
  Each Fellow gets a line about whether they’d take it; most of them won’t, and the ones who might are honest about it.
- **Featuring:** Dalinar, Thor, Steve (paying off Chapter 3), Sevro.
- **Choice:** Morrow asks you directly. *“No.”* / *“Ask them, not me.”* / *“Not today.”* The last one is the joke and
  the trap: Morrow laughs for the first time, because that’s his word. Every choice leads to the same refusal,
  but the heroes remember which one you picked.

### Chapter 8: The Thief of Days
- **Unlock:** King Black Dragon defeated + total level 490 (Barrows).
- **Beats:** The King Black Dragon was the lock on the last and oldest rift, the one Morrow came through. Past it,
  in the grey, there’s no fight in the usual sense: Morrow can’t be hit, only starved. He walks with the player
  and talks, reasonable as ever, about everything they haven’t done yet. The heroes hold the rift open behind you
  while the player answers him the only way that works: by pointing back at the village, at every promise kept since
  Chapter 1 (the game reads real stats here: total habits, longest streak, areas at 50+). The Hearth roars through the
  rift, it slams shut, and Morrow is sealed outside. He isn’t dead. He’ll still send heralds, and he still knocks on
  quiet evenings. But he’s thinner now, and Everstead is lit.
- **Featuring:** Everyone recruited gets one line at the rift; spoken scenes centre on Darrow, Obi-Wan and Maggie,
  closing the loop from Chapter 1.
- **Choice:** Morrow’s last question: “And tomorrow?” *“I’ll do it today.”* / *“Same as today.”* / your Chapter 1
  answer, quoted back. Whatever you pick becomes the line shown on the Hearth in the square from then on.
- **After:** Blights, the Rift, the Spire and the Arena carry on (Morrow keeps trying). An epilogue scene at total
  level 540 (Saradomin armour) has Saradomin himself visit the Hearth, which is about as close to a thank-you as a god
  gets.

## 6. “How they got here”: origin tidbits

Each character gets a 2–3 sentence tidbit, shown on their profile and in their join scene.

**The rule:**
1. **Sentence one: the moment.** Where they were in their own story when Morrow grabbed them, at the instant just
   before they were about to act. Pick a real-feeling beat from canon and paraphrase; never quote. Choose a moment
   that isn’t a death scene or a big spoiler, and that has a promise or intention hanging in it.
2. **Sentence two: the landing.** How they came to in Everstead, and one concrete, slightly funny detail about it
   (what came with them, what didn’t, what they did first).
3. **Optional sentence three:** what they think of it now, in their voice-in-spirit.
- **Gielinor natives** (Maggie, the Wise Old Man, Saradomin, Zilyana, Ellamaria, the Slayer Masters, Anna the Vampyre)
  weren’t pulled through anything. Their tidbit says why they’re in Everstead instead: they walked, were sent, or
  noticed the fire.
- **Original characters** (Lys, Ariane, Briget, Megan, Fannie and the like) were in Everstead already or came for their
  own reasons; their tidbit says what drew them to the Hearth.
- Fellows’ tidbits stay comradely and Family tidbits stay neutral here; the romance lives in Moments, not origins.
- Keep each line short (under 160 characters), specific and a little dry.

**Examples:**

- **Steve Rogers (Marvel):** Morrow took him from the cockpit of a plane going down into Arctic ice, with a radio in his
  hand and a dance he’d promised someone on the other end. He woke up in Everstead’s square still holding the radio. He
  hasn’t switched it off.

- **Kelsier (Mistborn):** He was on a Luthadel rooftop with ash coming down, a coin just leaving his fingers, about to
  start the best job of his life. He landed in Everstead flat on his back. The coin turned up three days later, in
  Maggie’s soup.

- **Kaladin and Syl (The Stormlight Archive):** Kaladin was at the front of a bridge, running at a chasm, when the
  world went grey. Syl came too, because she was holding on to him and refused to let go. She describes the trip as
  “fun.” He doesn’t.

- **Geralt of Rivia (The Witcher):** He was reaching for a contract notice on a village board in Velen. He came to in
  Everstead with the notice still in his hand and no horse. He asks the Wise Old Man about Roach roughly once a week.

- **Grog Strongjaw (Critical Role):** Grog was mid-arm-wrestle in a tavern, about to win, when the table vanished. He
  arrived in Everstead with his arm still up and his elbow on nothing. He considers the match a win by forfeit.

- **Jaina Proudmoore (Warcraft):** She was on the deck of her flagship off Kul Tiras, staff raised, about to call the
  storm off her fleet. She arrived soaked, furious and already working out how the rift was built. She has notes. Many.

- **Darrow of Lykos (Red Rising):** He was deep in the mines under Lykos, clawdrill mid-turn, about to beat the
  quota and win the Laurel for his clan. He woke swinging at the Hearth. The first thing he asked was which Gold had
  done this; the second was whether Everstead has a training yard.

- **The Wise Old Man (Old School RuneScape, a native):** Nobody pulled him anywhere; he walked over from Draynor when he
  noticed the fire was going out. Somewhere on the road a stray rift took forty years off him. He has no intention of
  giving them back.
